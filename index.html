Research question and evidence

Project guide

Research question

How well can regression models represent the measured current–voltage (I–V) relationship of a 1N4004 diode, and how do measured I–V curves for selected silicon and silicon-carbide power devices vary with voltage and temperature?

What does one row represent?

In the 1N4004 table, one row is one measured voltage/current pair from a diode sweep. In the classified device table, one row is one measured voltage/current point for a named device at a stated temperature. Rows are measurement points, not separate physical devices.

The classified file used by the app contains measured silicon and SiC rows.

Variables and preprocessing

The principal variables are measured voltage (V), measured current (A), device identifier, material, and temperature (K). The 1N4004 loader retains the voltage and current columns, removes rows with missing values, and sorts measurements by voltage. The classified-data loader checks for the required columns and removes rows missing device, voltage, current, or temperature. For material-model prediction, the application selects positive-current observations at the selected device and exact measured temperature.

The source documentation flags readings below approximately 50 nA for cautious interpretation. The external review table also preserves near-zero negative readings with review notes rather than silently changing them. The classified table has 26 non-positive current rows; the predictor excludes these when constructing its material-model prediction subset.

Models and baseline

The displayed 1N4004 comparison includes Random Forest and tuned SVR, with Linear Regression and a mean-prediction baseline as reference models. The application also loads device/material SVR estimators for silicon and silicon-carbide measurements. These saved estimators are used for interactive inference; this page does not claim that the multi-device estimators were retrained during an app session.

Evaluation and recorded results

The following metrics are stored in the application as previously recorded results for a random holdout from the 1N4004 measurement sweep. MAE and RMSE are in amperes; lower is better. R² closer to 1 indicates a closer fit on the reported test set.

ModelMAE (A)RMSE (A)R²

On the reported metrics, tuned SVR has the lowest RMSE and highest R², while Random Forest has the lowest MAE. This means the ranking depends on the error measure: SVR has the smaller squared-error penalty overall, but Random Forest has the smaller average absolute error. Both reported machine-learning models outperform the displayed baselines on these stored values.

Reproducibility gap: the submitted repository does not include the original training notebooks/scripts, exact random split indices, split sizes, or per-observation residuals for these metrics. The metrics are therefore reported as stored results, not independently recomputed here. The exact split configuration and detailed error analysis must be added from the original experiment records before claiming full reproduction.

Limitations

  • The reported 1N4004 scores come from a random holdout within one measured sweep; nearby points may be correlated.
  • The available classified silicon and silicon-carbide measurements cover 77 K and 293 K only. Predictions at unmeasured temperatures are not supported by the dataset interface.
  • Device-specific measurements are sparse compared with large-scale datasets, and device coverage is uneven.
  • Model performance outside the measured voltage, temperature, and device conditions is not established.
  • The repository currently lacks the exact training workflow and split artifacts needed to independently reproduce all reported scores.
